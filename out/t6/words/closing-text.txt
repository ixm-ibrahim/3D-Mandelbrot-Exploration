H2: Questions from testing, answered
H3: Why did the Sphere inversions solid come apart when it was sliced away from z = 0?
P: The version you tested had Planes at 2. That setting copies the flat rule onto a second plane, at right angles to the first. The rule then jumps across the plane y = 0, and the jump tears the solid unless c lies on the x-axis. On z = 0 nothing of this shows, which is why that slice looked right. The page now opens with Planes at 1. That rule has no jump, and the solid is one smooth piece at every height.
H3: Should the number of planes be forced by the maths?
P: Yes, and with one plane it is. Ask three things of a rule for space: it squares lengths, its slice at z = 0 is the flat set, and it keeps angles true on the sphere of directions. One rule is left, with its mirror image, and it is the one the page opens with. The mirror image has an answer of its own further down. That is a short argument of ours, not a published theorem. The rule gives the “inflated” sets of Figure 3 in Kučera’s paper: one smooth piece at every height, with detail around one plane only.
H3: Should the plane come from where the point starts?
P: That idea is in the list as the Lathe approach: every point uses the plane through the x-axis and itself. It is forced too, and it is clean. It gives rings where one hopes for beads.
H3: Where is the look of Figure 4, with beads around two circles?
P: In Kučera’s paper Figure 4 exists only for c on the real axis, which here is the x-axis. The paper says so: “so far the second construction is unfortunately limited to c ∈ R” (page 444). On this page the same picture appears with Planes at 2 and c on the x-axis, because a mirror symmetry of the set hides the jump.
DIV: [Show Figure 4] [Back to one plane]
P: Show Figure 4 goes to the Julia set for c = (−1, 0, 0) at power 2, under Sphere inversions with Planes at 2, and frames it. It also switches off what would hide that solid or change it: the 2D view, the cut, the modifiers and a trap that is the solid. Back to one plane sets Planes to 1 again.
P: Six new rules were tried, in two rounds, to get that look for every c without a jump: three “pillow” rules, a smooth blend of the two planes, bulbs about the x-axis, and a pillow that keeps angles true. Judged by their pictures, none works. A rule that is exact on two planes at right angles and never jumps has to fold the sphere of directions over itself, or wind it three times round the negative x-axis. The first gives a swollen mass and dust. The second gives rocks and fibres. So a Figure 4 for every c, and with it a Figure 4 Mandelbrot solid, is an open problem.
H3: Is there a solid with no seam that is 3D all the way through?
P: The nearest thing found is one tick away. Every rule has a mirror twin: take the same step, then swap up and down, so that z becomes −z. Under Modifiers that is the tick box “flip z”. Points of the plane z = 0 do not notice the swap, so the slice at z = 0 is still the flat set. For Sphere inversions and the Lathe, a Julia set whose c lies in that plane does not change at all: the pictures match pixel for pixel. The Mandelbrot solid does change, because there c leaves the plane.
DIV: [Twin of Sphere inversions] [Twin of the Lathe] [Twin of the Mandelbulb] [Back to the plain rule]
P: Under Sphere inversions the thin lens becomes a tall body, and its slices keep the outline of the Mandelbrot set far above the plane. Tick 2D view and move “Height z of the cut or slice” to see them. The plain rule pushes a point away from the plane the same way at every step, so its solid thins to nothing at the edge of the flat set. The twin turns the push round each time, and the solid stands tall. This twin is the mirror image named above: the other rule that squares lengths, keeps the flat slice and keeps angles true.
P: The twin of the Lathe has the Mandelbrot set in the plane z = 0 and its three-cornered cousin, the tricorn, in the plane y = 0, joined without a seam. The twin of the Mandelbulb is fins and feathers in every direction. None of the three is Figure 4, which wants the same flat set in two planes. Each button shows the Mandelbrot solid at power 2 with the approach’s own dials at their starting values, and switches off what would hide that solid or change it. Back to the plain rule unticks flip z.
H3: Do quaternions or the third map have a part in this?
P: “Use the third map”, in the Kleinian group section, is the same kind of move as the third map in Kučera’s paper: it is what lets a Kleinian group fill space instead of one plane. It is not the paper’s group, and the squaring rules have no such switch. Quaternions are how the Kleinian maps are written down. Squaring a quaternion is in the list too, as the Lathe approach.
H3: Why did the orbit trap only recolour the surface in 3D?
P: Because the trap was used for colour only. Colour and light now has a drop-down, “In the 3D view the trap”, with three choices: Colours the surface, Is the solid, and Is added to the solid. With Is the solid, the object is made of every point whose orbit comes within the Trap thickness of the trap shape, so each Trap shape gives a different object. “Orbit steps skipped at the start” peels the large outer pieces away. This works in Mandelbrot, Julia and Kleinian pictures, and the solid keeps its colours at every quality setting. With the Sphere shell, a Mandelbrot picture starts as plain balls: try the cross or the ring as Trap shape, or raise the skipped steps to 2 or 3. The 2D view is as it was.
H3: Does the 2D view use a distance estimate, and can the inside have colours of its own?
P: Yes to both. The outline of the flat picture is drawn from the same distance estimate the 3D rays use, which is why thin threads show as clean lines. With 2D view ticked, Colour and light shows three more controls: “Outline width, pixels”, “Glow beside the outline, pixels” and “Inside the set, colour by”. The inside can stay Plain, or be coloured by Orbit trap, Where the orbit ends up, How deep inside, or How quickly the orbit settles, and “Palette for the inside” gives it colours of its own. Until you move these the 2D picture looks as it did. In the 3D view, with the solid cut open, the tick box “Colour the cut face like the inside of the 2D view” does what it says.
H3: Was the glow around the solid with the rough distance estimate a fault?
P: Yes, and it has been fixed. A ray that ran alongside the solid crept forward in tiny steps, ran out of steps in mid-air, and was then shaded as if it had hit something. Such rays now get past the solid, and a ray that runs out of steps away from the surface shows the background. The new trap solids were checked for it too.
H3: Why did solids look like a stack of slices, even at Fine?
P: The slices were in the colouring, not in the shape: drawn in one plain colour, the same surface was smooth. With the orbit trap, the colour of each point was read from more steps of its orbit than a pixel can show. The number of steps changed from pixel to pixel, and that painted one ring for every step. The last few steps now fade out of the colouring. This is cured for the opening pictures of Mandelbrot and Julia sets, and the Kleinian view never had it. It is not finished. With another Trap radius faint rings can come back, and the colours a trap gives a surface still shift with the quality setting and with the Iterations. The next version reads a fixed number of orbit steps, from a point on the surface itself, to end that. The price so far is a calmer picture than before. A Glow width of 2 or 3 brings more colour back.
H3: What does the page open with?
P: With what you asked for: the Balanced quality, Colour by at Orbit trap, the Sphere shell as Trap shape, “How near it came, as a glow”, the Rainbow palette reversed, and the Kleinian Depth of the search at 150, the top of its slider.
H3: How far can the dials go?
P: Extreme ranges, at the top of the controls, lets most dials reach five to ten times further: Iterations, Depth of the search, “Ray steps, at most”, Refining passes, Power n and more. A dial with a wider range shows the word wide while the switch is on. Switching it off brings every value back inside its usual limits.
H3: What does a list entry change?
P: Only what it is about. A Julia entry sets the point c. A Kleinian entry sets the group and its turning-out sphere. The camera, the iterations, the depth of the search, the quality and the approach stay as you left them. Frame it, beside each list, moves the camera to a good view of the chosen entry. As it was found, beside the Julia list, sets the planes and the iterations the entry was found with, and shows only while yours differ.
P: The Julia points are a drop-down now, like the Kleinian list, with your saved points at the top. Six entries look like their names only with Planes at 2, and they stand under “Found with two planes”.
H3: Are there Kleinian entries that move the turning-out sphere?
P: There were none, and that was not intended. The list now has 15 of them under “Turning-out sphere”, and nothing was taken away. They keep groups that were already in the list and move only the sphere.
H3: How do I get a picture or a 3D model out?
P: Save a picture draws the view as it is when you press Start the picture, at the Size, Quality and File type you choose, while you carry on. Changes made afterwards do not reach a picture that is already being made. When it is ready, press Save the file.
P: Save a 3D model turns the solid into a mesh of triangles and packs it in a zip as STL, OBJ and PLY files, which Blender, Rhino and 3D printing programs open. Press Start the model, then Save the file.
P: One step is untested. On the published page a file reaches you through the page that hosts the lab, which asks you to confirm each file. That could not be tried on the test bench, so the first real test of saving a file from the published page is yours. If a save fails, the lab says so and keeps the file, and you can press Save the file again.
H3: Why does the picture wait after I choose another approach or equation?
P: Each approach and each equation needs a drawing program of its own, and so do the Kleinian view and a power other than 2. A trap that is the solid needs one too, and so do colours for the inside of the set. The browser takes a moment to prepare a program the first time it is needed, and the picture says “Preparing the formula” meanwhile. After that it is kept, so going back is quick.

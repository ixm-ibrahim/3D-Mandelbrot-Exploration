Written by the judge, who did not do the work. Every sentence is marked **measured** (a number I computed again with

Evidence, numbers and internal names are in `NOTES.md` beside this file.

## Can two planes and along be ironed into one rule?

1. Not in the way those two rules work, and that part is a proof. **reasoned** (round 21's proof, checked again by the mathematics section)
2. Both get their buds from extra planes through the x-axis, each showing its own true copy of the flat picture. **measured** in round 22
3. Such a second plane can exist only while c lies on the x-axis; for the rabbit there is none, whatever the rule. **reasoned** (the proof)
4. That is why, off the x-axis, two planes breaks into shards and along swells into one lump. **seen** in picture 1; **measured** (a tenth of two planes' rabbit is thin scrap and some of it floats; along's rabbit is one piece three and a half times one plane's size)
5. By any other way it is not known; every rule we built or found that grows bodies off the flat picture is lopsided, or crusted, or makes a lump of the rabbit. **measured** and **seen** for the ten rules I ran again
6. No rule known to us, ours or anyone's, gives the rabbit round buds all over in the flat picture's own spacing. **seen**; "anyone's" rests on the 17 outside formulas that could be pinned down

## What has anyone else found?

1. Others reached the same two looks by the same idea: Msltoe's formula (about 2010) is the two-planes look and Eiffie's (2015) is the along look, for c = -1. **measured** (their shapes differ from ours on 4 % and 2.5 % of the inside); **seen** in picture 2
2. Off the x-axis theirs are worse than ours: Msltoe's has no rabbit at all, Eiffie's rabbit is one lump, and neither shows the right flat picture there. **measured**; **seen** in picture 2
3. Of the 17 outside formulas, seven keep the flat picture exactly, and none of those has round buds off it together with a clean rabbit: the Mandelbulb at power 2 is flakes and whiskers, the quaternion square is rings, Bristorbrot is ridges. **measured** (flat picture, rings); **seen** for the Mandelbulb and Bristorbrot in picture 2, the other four by the outside section only
4. In mathematics the one-plane rule turns out to be a published rule, nothing published draws a 3D z^2 + c with buds all over, and the quaternion square can only spin the flat picture round the x-axis. The first two are the mathematics section's reading of the papers (I did not read them); the third is **measured** (0 of 300,000 points change when turned)

## What does the best new try look like, and what is wrong with it?

1. It shows the flat picture a second time, rolled up into a cone above and below the flat one; used for the first step only it gives smooth round extra bodies that touch the middle body at single points, on the rabbit too. **measured** (the cone holds the flat picture exactly; nothing floats; the bodies are at most 1.4 times as long as thick); **seen** in picture 1
2. On the rabbit that is better than along once, whose biggest extra body floats free; on c = -1 it is behind along: extra bodies in 6 places round the middle body against along's 8, none on the other bodies against along's 16, and they lean to one side. **measured**
3. Used at every step, as a real rule must be, it buries everything under scales and loose flakes, and the Siegel disc slowly leaks away. **seen** in pictures 1 and 3; **measured** (a fifth of the rabbit is thin scrap; the Siegel disc loses a quarter of its volume between 30 and 200 steps)
4. And it is as arbitrary as along once: a special first step, a Mandelbrot solid that is just one plane's, and extra bodies that are the flat picture's own bodies again, not new bulbs. **measured**

## May be told

- For c off the x-axis no second plane can hold a true copy of the flat picture. This is a proof, and it is the reason the
  way of "two planes" and "along" cannot be carried to the rabbit.
- Above the rabbit's meeting point along stops lifting points where one plane keeps lifting them (height times 1.00
  against 1.10 at height 0.075). **measured** again.
- Others found the two-planes look (Msltoe, about 2010) and the along look (Eiffie, 2015) by the same idea. Ours are the
  better versions off the x-axis. Eiffie's formula and date were checked against the source of the program Mandelbulber.
- The Mandelbulb's own author writes that it is not the real thing.
- Quaternion rotations do not do it: the quaternion square of a point turned round the x-axis is the square turned the
  same way, so its shapes are the flat picture spun round (rings). **measured**
- A rule that reaches every spot from only two places and never folds space has no bodies off the flat picture (a
  proof from the mathematics section); the one-plane rule is that rule. **measured**: it lays the directions over each other exactly twice.
- The new try lays them over each other exactly four times without a fold, and keeps shapes on the flat plane and on
  the two cones. **measured**
- The new try for one step: smooth round bodies touching at single points, the same kind of contact the flat rabbit's
  own bodies have. For two steps: more bodies, some stretched 3 to 1. Three: thin blades. Every step: a crust.
- "Along" for the first 1, 2, 3 steps: the rabbit's bodies run together a little more with each step (picture 3).
- Both first-step tricks (along once and the new try) vanish when c is turned 3 degrees out of the flat picture, as
  one plane does, and both have one plane's Mandelbrot solid. **measured**
- Every rule shown is exact on the flat picture with a real body behind it, and the dendrite is empty under all of them. **measured**
- Along once does not always have a floating body: for one other c (a bud with four bodies round each meeting point) it has none. **measured**

## May NOT be told

- "It is proved that no rule can do it." Proved only for the way two planes and along do it. The rest is open.
- "A rule that turns the way the flat rule does can never have buds off the flat picture" (this round's briefing, 2 (b)).
  False unless "and never folds" is added: the mathematics section's counterexample holds (**measured** again: its
  45-degree cone holds the rabbit's flat picture exactly, and 93 % of it survives half a degree off the cone).
- "The new try is the answer", "is principled", or "beats along". It is behind along on c = -1 and is a first-step trick.
- "The new try makes new bulbs of 3D." Its extra bodies are the flat picture's own bodies, rolled up.
- "The new try's extra bodies hold on by thinner contacts than one plane's own bodies" (design section). Did not hold:
  the contact is the same to the last digit, and on my blocks one plane's own bodies fail the same strict test.
- "The new try has as many buds as along on c = -1." It has fewer, on one body only.
- "The crust is proved unavoidable." Not proved; it is the open question.
- "The square pillow's extra bodies are joined" (rabbit section). On finer blocks 24 of 26 only touch at a point, and a
  tenth of its rabbit is thin scrap.
- "The Mandelcup is the one-plane rule." Only at one dial setting that we chose; the program's own defaults were not read.
- "Msltoe, 2010" as an exact year, or anything quoted from the forum itself: the forum could not be read; the formulas
  come from the Mandelbulber source that cites it. Three forum threads are untested.
- "Nobody anywhere has found it." Say: none of the 17 formulas we could pin down.
- "Bristorbrot has two exact planes." Each of its two planes is exact only for a c that lies in it.
- Any cut picture of the new try as proof of clean bodies. The cuts look cleaner than the solids.
- A count such as "8 extra bodies on the rabbit": the count depends on how small a body is counted (I get 6 per half
  on finer blocks, 2 of them big). Say "a handful".

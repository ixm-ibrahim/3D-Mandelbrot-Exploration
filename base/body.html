3D Squaring Lab

In the plane, squaring a point squares its length and doubles its angle. A point in space has two angles and no agreed way to double them, so every 3D Mandelbrot starts by choosing an approach. Choose one here, give it any equation, and check which properties of the flat set it keeps.

One Julia set belongs to each point c of the Mandelbrot set. Kučera’s paper draws c = (−1, 0, 0), the Basilica, seen straight down the z-axis.

A true 3D Kleinian group: three shape-keeping maps of space, written with quaternions as q → q + 2, q → q + 2L·j and q → μ + 1/q, where q = x + yi + zj. The solid is every sphere those maps can carry two flat walls onto. The picture is turned inside out through one sphere so that it fits on the stage.

Without the third map the pearls sit along one line, as in the paper’s “inflated” figure. With it they spread in every direction. The quaternion μ sets how far apart the walls are (its i part), how the pearls lean (real part) and how they lean in depth (j part). Only some values of μ give a clean set. Elsewhere the pearls overlap and the picture turns to dust.

Why no approach passes every row

In the plane, squaring stretches every direction by the same factor, 2|z|. Doubling the angle stretches the circle of directions by 2, and squaring the length stretches outward by the same amount, so tiny shapes keep their shape. That is the source of the endless clean detail in the flat set.

To copy this in space, a rule would have to stretch the sphere of directions by exactly 2 at every point. A sphere cannot be laid over itself that way: a patch magnified 2× is flatter than the sphere it has to land on. Liouville proved the general statement in 1850: in three dimensions the only shape-keeping maps are built from rotations, scalings and sphere inversions, and squaring is not one of them.

That is also why Kleinian groups look the way a 3D Julia set ought to. They are built from sphere inversions alone, the one family the theorem allows, so they keep their detail in every direction. Quaternions enter as bookkeeping: 1/q is a sphere inversion that stays inside 3D, where q² only gives a lathe. An approach that must contain the exact flat set at z = 0 cannot be built from inversions near that plane, which is why the inversion pictures live under Kleinian and not in the list of approaches.
